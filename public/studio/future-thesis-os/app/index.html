    Method

    How this model works

    It does not predict a certain future, but simultaneously maintains multiple possible futures, and continuously asks: which is becoming more probable, why, and what signals would falsify it.

    Probability is belief strength, not frequency

    The world will only run once, so "0.76" is not "76% will happen", but the model's current belief strength that the proposition is true. It does not pursue absolute accuracy, but requires comparability: a belief strength of 0.76 must truly be stronger than 0.62.

    The weekly revision magnitude is restricted to a very small range, unless a pre-registered event truly happens and triggers an adjudication.

    What would prove it wrong

    Every judgement has registered falsification conditions, divided into three categories: Class A will be adjudicated by a specific event within a foreseeable time; Class B is a tripwire, which once triggered only weakens and does not strengthen; Class C is a long-cycle open proposition, continuously increasing or decreasing belief strength according to evidence.

    How it absorbs the world

    Every Saturday morning, an autonomously running programme reads the industry news, research, and signals of the week, finds out which evidence hits existing judgements and which structural trends are not yet in the map, and then revises them one by one. Every modification is written into an append-only evolution log, therefore the full history of any belief can be reconstructed week by week.

    What it does not do

    It does not look at stock prices, holds no stances, and gives no investment advice. It is isolated in data from any market-perspective systems, and only deduces the future starting from structural changes of the world. Calibration only measures "whether confidence tiers correlate with hit rates", and does not measure profit and loss.
